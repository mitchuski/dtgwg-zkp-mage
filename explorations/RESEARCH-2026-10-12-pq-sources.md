Compiled 2026-10-12 for the ToIP DTG ZKP Task Force. Each claim carries: source URL, the date I believe the source is from, and a confidence tag:
`confirmed-primary` = read in the primary document (page or extracted PDF text); `secondary` = from search summaries, blogs, or abstracts I could not open in full; `uncertain` = conflicting or thin evidence.

Primary texts quoted were extracted from the cited PDFs at compile time (NIST IR 8547 ipd, ETSI TR 119 476-1 V1.3.1, the IETF 124 Longfellow dispatch slides); re-check quotes against the URLs given.

## 1. Why Groth16 / KZG / pairing SNARKs are not post-quantum, and *which* property breaks

1.1 **Groth16 is a 3-group-element pairing SNARK whose soundness is argued in the generic bilinear group model.** Groth, "On the Size of Pairing-based Non-interactive Arguments", ePrint 2016/260 (EUROCRYPT 2016, rev. May 2016). https://eprint.iacr.org/2016/260 — `confirmed-primary` (abstract page; PDF returned 403). The standard reading — perfect zero-knowledge, computational knowledge soundness in the GGM — is restated by zkSecurity, https://blog.zksecurity.xyz/posts/groth16/ (2024) — `secondary`.

1.2 **Shor breaks the discrete log in the pairing groups (BN254, BLS12-381), so the CRS trapdoor (τ, α, β, γ, δ) becomes recoverable from the public setup elements and proofs can be forged for false statements.** This is a consequence of 1.1 plus Shor; I found no paper dedicated to "quantum attacks on Groth16", only derivative statements, e.g. ZK-ACE, arXiv 2603.07974 (Mar 2026), which calls pairing-based SNARK soundness quantum-vulnerable. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.07974 — `secondary` (the mechanism is inference from 1.1; the conclusion is uncontroversial).

1.3 **What breaks is soundness/authenticity, not zero-knowledge.** Perfect (information-theoretic) ZK holds against unbounded adversaries by definition, so a CRQC gains nothing about hidden witnesses from a Groth16 proof. The same split is stated normatively for BBS: "the BBS Signatures scheme is based on the discrete logarithm problem. This means that it is not 'post-quantum secure'. However, the privacy and hiding properties of BBS proofs are resilient even against an attacker utilizing a Cryptographically Relevant Quantum Computer"; §6.9 calls this "everlasting privacy" and notes it covers BBS *proofs*, not BBS *signatures*. draft-irtf-cfrg-bbs-signatures-12 (28 Sep 2026). https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-irtf-cfrg-bbs-signatures — `confirmed-primary`.

1.4 **ETSI's formal statement of the same split, for every credential family.** ETSI TR 119 476-1 V1.3.1 (Aug 2025) clause 9.1: "a quantum adversary will be able to break the authenticity of QEAA systems but not (in most cases) their privacy ... all QEAA systems achieving unconditional privacy are immune to the store now decrypt later attack and so could postpone their transition as long as it is completed before the Q-day." Clause 9.1 item 1 lists BBS+, BBS#, CL, PS-MS and KVAC as unconditionally private, but warns the property "only results from a careful design ... Any modification ... might then affect this property" (DAA variants lose it). Executive summary: for programmable ZKPs "it depends on the design of the arithmetic circuit proof if soundness holds in the presence of a quantum computer. For example, the hash-based NARKs used in [i.113 = Frigo–shelat Longfellow] are considered post-quantum secure in this regard, while others (like the ones used in [i.65], [i.182], [i.269]) are not as they rely on elliptic curves." https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_tr/119400_119499/11947601/01.03.01_60/tr_11947601v010301p.pdf — `confirmed-primary` (extracted text).

1.5 **"Harvest now, forge later" vs "harvest now, decrypt later".** ETSI clause 9.2: HNDL is a confidentiality threat and does not apply to unconditionally-hiding proofs; signature/proof forging "is arguably more relevant ... Here, the risk is relatively much lower due to the time frames involved" because an attacker needs public material first and cannot pre-collect; mitigations are "frequent key rotation ... short-lived attestations ... one-time signing and proof keys", then migration to ML-DSA and "post-quantum safe zero-knowledge solutions". Same URL — `confirmed-primary`.

1.6 **Are already-verified proofs at risk retroactively?** Not as verifications already performed; the risk is (a) *re-verification* of archived proofs/signatures after Q-day and (b) a forger back-dating. ETSI 9.2 gives the worked example of a signed, time-stamped, hash-chained archive: the attacker can derive the signer's key but cannot rewrite the time-stamped chain — i.e. trusted timestamps / hash chains are the protection for long-lived evidence. Same URL — `confirmed-primary`. Cloudflare's PQ overview frames signature migration as "less urgent, as we only need to have everyone migrated by the time the cryptographically-relevant quantum computer arrives" and flags SLH-DSA for "long-lasting signatures" (Mar 2024). https://blog.cloudflare.com/pq-2024/ — `confirmed-primary`.

1.7 **Regulatory clock on the pairing/EC assumptions.** NIST IR 8547 ipd (Nov 2024), Table 2: ECDSA, EdDSA and RSA at 112-bit strength "Deprecated after 2030"; all parameter sets "Disallowed after 2035"; §4.1.3: symmetric primitives and hashes at ≥128-bit classical security "are believed to meet ... Category 1" (i.e. hash-based ZK survives). The draft quotes NSM-10's 2035 goal. https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2024/NIST.IR.8547.ipd.pdf — `confirmed-primary` (extracted text). Note: BLS12-381 and BN254 do not appear in NIST's lists at all; they are non-NIST curves and would fall under the same "disallowed after 2035" logic by analogy — `uncertain`.

1.8 **Extraction caveat for a PQ reader.** Groth16 knowledge soundness is proved via GGM/AGM extraction; the generic-group restriction is not obviously meaningful for a quantum adversary, which is a separate (theoretical) reason not to claim any quantum soundness for Groth16 even at parameters a CRQC cannot yet break. Source: the generic-operation restriction in Groth16 and in "Extending Groth16 for Disjunctive Statements", ePrint 2025/028 (Jun 2025). https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/028 — `secondary`.

## 2. Hash-based transparent proof systems and their PQ status

2.1 **Theory: IOP-based SNARGs with round-by-round soundness are unconditionally secure in the QROM.** Chiesa–Manohar–Spooner, "Succinct Arguments in the Quantum Random Oracle Model", ePrint 2019/834 (TCC 2019, rev. Jan 2020). https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/834 — `confirmed-primary`. This is the result that covers the BCS/Fiat–Shamir compilation of STARK/Aurora/Ligero-style IOPs, provided the IOP is round-by-round sound.

2.2 **Kilian's hash-based succinct argument is post-quantum secure in the standard model from any collapsing hash.** Chiesa–Ma–Spooner–Zhandry, ePrint 2021/334 (FOCS 2021). https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/334 — `confirmed-primary`.

2.3 **FRI under Fiat–Shamir.** Block, Garreta, Katz, Thaler, Tiwari, Zając, "Fiat-Shamir Security of FRI and Related SNARKs", ePrint 2023/1071 (ASIACRYPT 2023, rev. Mar 2024): proves FS security of FRI and batched FRI; proves round-by-round knowledge soundness for Plonky2 "under the assumption that adversaries always send low-degree polynomials"; sketches ethSTARK and RISC Zero. The published abstract states FRI via FS is unconditionally secure in the QROM. https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1071 and https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-99-8724-5_1 — `confirmed-primary` (ePrint page); QROM sentence `secondary` (Springer abstract via search).

2.4 **The 2025–26 proximity-gaps shock (parameter, not break).** Crites–Stewart, "On Reed–Solomon Proximity Gaps Conjectures", ePrint 2025/2046 (Dec 2025): the up-to-capacity correlated-agreement / mutual-correlated-agreement / list-decodability conjectures are false. Diamond–Gruen, ePrint 2025/2010 (Nov 2025) give explicit counterexamples (authors of Binius). Ben-Sasson, Carmon, Haböck, Kopparty, Saraf, ePrint 2025/2055 (Dec 2025). Kambiré, arXiv 2604.09724 (Apr 2026): failure O(1/log n) below capacity over prime fields. Deployed FRI/STIR/WHIR systems that set parameters "up to capacity" are affected. zkSecurity's analysis: moving to the restated conjecture costs ~2–3 % proof size; moving to *proven* bounds roughly doubles proof size/verifier time; small-field systems are most exposed. https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2046.pdf , https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2055.pdf , https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09724 , https://blog.zksecurity.xyz/posts/proximity-conjecture/ — `secondary` (abstracts/analysis via search; I did not open the PDFs). Spec implication: any PQ profile should cite *proven* proximity bounds or name the conjecture it relies on.

2.5 **Current hash-based building blocks (identifiers).** FRI (Ben-Sasson et al., ICALP 2018); Ligero (Ames–Hazay–Ishai–Venkitasubramaniam, CCS 2017; ePrint 2022/1608); Aurora (ePrint 2018/828); Brakedown (ePrint 2021/1043); STIR (ePrint 2024/390); WHIR (Arnon–Chiesa–Fenzi–Yogev, ePrint 2024/1586, Oct–Nov 2024: for a 2^22 polynomial at 100-bit security, 63 KiB opening, 360 µs verification) https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1586 — `confirmed-primary` for WHIR, `secondary` for the others (not fetched). Binius: Diamond–Posen, "Succinct Arguments over Towers of Binary Fields", ePrint 2023/1784 (rev. Apr 2025), Brakedown-style multilinear PCS over GF(2^k) towers; the abstract makes no PQ claim. https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1784 — `confirmed-primary`. The PQ status of all of these rests on 2.1–2.3 (hash + QROM) plus the proximity-gap parameterisation in 2.4.

2.6 **Proof sizes vs Groth16.** Groth16: 3 group elements = 128 bytes on BN254 (192 on BLS12-381), constant. Measured hash-based "shrink"-stage proofs (ePrint 2026/2383, 2026): SP1 ≈ 870 KiB, Stwo ≈ 533 KiB, RISC Zero ≈ 218 KiB, all labelled "Not post-quantum secure" once Groth16-wrapped. https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2383.pdf — `secondary`. ProveKit (Spartan+WHIR) proofs ≈ 1 MB on-device (World, Sep 2026) — `secondary` (see 7.6). zkDilithium STARK tokens 85–175 KB — `secondary` (see 3.9). LaBRADOR lattice proof 58 KB for 2^20 R1CS — `confirmed-primary` (3.2). Order of magnitude: PQ proofs are 10^2–10^4 × Groth16.

## 3. Lattice-based ZK and anonymous credentials

3.1 **Σ-protocols over lattices (the "LNP22" framework).** Lyubashevsky–Nguyen–Plançon, "Lattice-Based Zero-Knowledge Proofs and Applications: Shorter, Simpler, and More General", ePrint 2022/284 (CRYPTO 2022): proofs of knowledge of short s with As = t mod q, exact ℓ2 norm, over Z[X]/(X^n+1); group signatures/verifiable encryption "more than twice as compact as the previously best". https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/284 — `confirmed-primary`.

3.2 **LaBRADOR.** Beullens–Seiler, "LaBRADOR: Compact Proofs for R1CS from Module-SIS", ePrint 2022/1341 (CRYPTO 2023): 58 KB proof for a 2^20-constraint R1CS mod 2^64+1, "an order of magnitude smaller than previous quantum-safe proofs"; verifier is linear (not succinct). https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1341 — `confirmed-primary`.

3.3 **Greyhound.** Nguyen–Seiler, "Greyhound: Fast Polynomial Commitments from Lattices", ePrint 2024/1293 (CRYPTO 2024): lattice PCS, composes with LaBRADOR for sublinear verification. https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1293.pdf — `secondary`.

3.4 **Jeudy–Roux-Langlois–Sanders, "Lattice Signature with Efficient Protocols, Application to Anonymous Credentials", ePrint 2022/509 (CRYPTO 2023, rev. Aug 2025):** first lattice AC; proof of message–signature possession < 650 KB; page notes it is superseded by Argo et al. https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/509 — `confirmed-primary`.

3.5 **Argo, Güneysu, Jeudy, Land, Roux-Langlois, Sanders, "Practical Post-Quantum Signatures for Privacy", ePrint 2024/131 (CCS 2024, rev. Aug 2025):** lattice SEP + full AC implementation; credentials < 80 KB (79.58 KB), improved to 53.98 KB with the Asiacrypt 2025 sampler (ePrint 2024/1952). https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/131 — `confirmed-primary`. (ETSI TR 119 476-1 §9.4.2 tracks exactly this lineage Libert 2016 → Jeudy 2023 → Argo 2024 as the PQ-AC state of the art — `confirmed-primary`.)

3.6 **Bootle–Lyubashevsky–Nguyen–Sorniotti, "A Framework for Practical Anonymous Credentials from Lattices", ePrint 2023/560 (CRYPTO 2023):** blind signatures, AC and group signatures from one framework; proofs "as small as a few dozen kilobytes" for up to 2^128 users; new "ISIS-with-hints"-style assumption family. https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/560 — `confirmed-primary`. Follow-up: Nguyen–Siemer on SIS-with-hints assumptions, PKC 2026 — `secondary`.

3.7 **LaZer library.** Lyubashevsky–Seiler–Steuer, "The LaZer Library: Lattice-Based Zero Knowledge and Succinct Proofs for Quantum-Safe Privacy", ePrint 2024/1846 (CCS 2024): C + Python; generates proof systems from declared relations/norm bounds; ships blind signatures, anonymous credentials, Kyber-key PoK, aggregate signatures; uses LaBRADOR for succinct proofs or LNP22 linear-size proofs. https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1846 — `confirmed-primary`. Revocation on top of the LaZer AC: Kemmoe–Lysyanskaya–Nguyen, "Lattice-Based Accumulator and Application to Anonymous Credential Revocation", ePrint 2025/1099 (2025). https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1099 — `secondary`.

3.8 **Lattice blind signatures.** Beullens–Lyubashevsky–Nguyen–Seiler, ePrint 2023/077 (Jan 2023): 2-round, 22 KB signatures, 60 KB transcript, Module-SIS/LWE + NTRU; keyed-verification variant with 48-byte signatures. https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/077 — `confirmed-primary`. BRaccoon (blind signatures from Raccoon), ePrint 2026/1084 (2026). https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1084.pdf — `secondary`. Other lattice-AC items 2025–26: traceable AC from module lattices, IACR CiC (Jan 2026), ≈317 KB credentials, https://cic.iacr.org/p/2/4/12 — `secondary`.

3.9 **zkDilithium (a "ZK-friendly PQ signature" by modification).** Policharla, Westerbaan, Faz-Hernández, Wood, "Post-Quantum Privacy Pass via Post-Quantum Anonymous Credentials", ePrint 2023/414 (2023): Dilithium2 with SHAKE replaced by Poseidon so a STARK can prove knowledge of a signature; tokens 85–175 KB, 0.3–5 s at ~115-bit proof security; Rust/winterfell prototype "not production-ready"; browser demo. https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/414 , https://zkdilithium.cloudflareresearch.com/ — `secondary`.

3.10 **Standards status: there is no PQ anonymous-credential or PQ-BBS draft at IETF/CFRG or W3C.** CFRG BBS is draft-12 (Sep 2026), explicitly non-PQ (1.3). W3C CCG Data Integrity calls discussed "post-quantum secure BBS pseudonyms ... everlasting privacy" (Apr–Jun 2025 minutes) and in Jan 2026 recorded that PQ cryptosuite progress "has been slow due to a lack of dedicated editors". https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-credentials/2025Apr/0059.html , https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-credentials/2026Jan/0050.html — `secondary`. ETSI §9.4.1: "there are not yet any similar PQS standardization efforts for blind signatures, group signatures, and anonymous credentials" — `confirmed-primary`. Maturity caveat: a 2025 survey says lattice ACs are "poorly studied for security and have only proof-of-concept implementations", arXiv 2501.07209 — `secondary`.

## 4. NIST PQC signature standards and the additional-signature on-ramp

4.1 **FIPS 204 (ML-DSA) and FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA)** were published 13 Aug 2024 and are the signature targets in NIST IR 8547 Table 3. https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/204/final , https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/205/final — `confirmed-primary` (NIST IR 8547 text references them; FIPS pages not re-opened).

4.2 **FIPS 206 (FN-DSA / Falcon) — still no Initial Public Draft as of 2026-10-12.** https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/206/ipd returns 404 (checked today). Secondary reports: draft submitted for approval 28 Aug 2025; NIST told pqc-forum in Nov 2025 it had no firm IPD date; final expected late 2026/2027. https://csrc.nist.gov/csrc/media/Presentations/2025/fips-206-fn-dsa-(falcon)/images-media/fips_206-perlner_2.1.pdf (NIST status talk, 2025) — `uncertain` on dates; `confirmed-primary` that no IPD page exists.

4.3 **Additional signatures Round 3.** NIST IR 8610, "Status Report on the Second Round of the Additional Digital Signature Schemes", final 14 May 2026: nine advance — FAEST (symmetric/VOLE-in-the-head), HAWK (lattice), MAYO, QR-UOV, SNOVA, UOV (multivariate), MQOM, SDitH (MPC-in-the-head), SQIsign (isogeny). The CSRC round-3 page (updated 28 Sep 2026) states HAWK has since been **withdrawn** by its team. https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8610/final , https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/pqc-dig-sig/round-3-additional-signatures — `confirmed-primary`. By subtraction from the 14 round-2 candidates, the eliminated five are CROSS, LESS, Mirath, PERK, RYDE — `secondary` (inferred). Round-3 tweaks due 14 Aug 2026; next PQC conference H1 2027 — `secondary`.

4.4 **What "ZK-friendly PQ signature" means (two different things).**
 (a) *The signature is itself a NIZK.* MPC-in-the-head / VOLE-in-the-head schemes are Fiat–Shamir'd ZK proofs of a one-way-function preimage: FAEST's site describes a signature as "a non-interactive zero-knowledge proof of knowledge of an AES key"; sizes 4,066 B (128s) to 20,856 B (256f). https://faest.info/ — `confirmed-primary`. MQOM and SDitH are the same paradigm over MQ / syndrome decoding. These compose conceptually with other ZK statements but are not succinct and are not designed for in-circuit verification.
 (b) *The signature verifies cheaply inside an arithmetic circuit.* Hash-based schemes instantiated with algebraic hashes: zkDilithium (Poseidon for SHAKE, 3.9); Miden's RPO-Falcon512 (RPO256 hash-to-point "more efficient to verify in Miden VM", 7.3); Ethereum's XMSS/Poseidon work (Drake–Khovratovich–Kudinov–Wagner, "Hash-Based Multi-Signatures for Post-Quantum Ethereum", ePrint 2025/055, CIC 2025, with aggregation by hash-based succinct arguments). https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/055 — `confirmed-primary`.
 **None of the schemes being standardised by NIST is designed for (b).** CNSA 2.0 prefers LMS/XMSS (SP 800-208) for firmware signing, which is the closest "SNARK-friendly-by-structure" standard family (hash-only), but with SHA-2/SHAKE, not algebraic hashes — `secondary`.

## 5. PQ signatures inside ZK proofs vs. "sign a commitment, prove membership"

5.1 **In-circuit ML-DSA costs (all self-reported, none peer-reviewed).** ML-DSA-87 verification in circom/Groth16: 16,518,893 constraints (Politecnico di Torino MSc, 2025) https://github.com/gabrielemorciano/dilithium5-poseidon — `secondary`. Dilithium verifier in SP1 zkVM: ~22 s proof, NTT ≈ 50 % of cycles, hashing ≈ 18 %, final 260-byte Groth16 wrapper (Medium, late 2025) https://medium.com/@phillyj1026/building-a-zero-knowledge-verifier-for-dilithium-signatures-ntt-gadget-implementation-in-sp1-6c50ab262836 — `secondary`. ML-DSA-44 in RISC Zero (Kaspa bench, mid-2026): ML-DSA is SHAKE-bound, Falcon NTT-bound, SLH-DSA SHA-256-bound https://github.com/KaspaKii/kaspa-pq-zk-bench — `secondary`. Analytical estimate "millions of R1CS constraints" for ML-DSA due to NTTs over degree-256 rings and 23-bit non-native modulus, ZK-ACE arXiv 2603.07974 (Mar 2026) — `secondary`. **No in-circuit numbers found for SLH-DSA or standard Falcon** (ePrint 2025/1741 measures on-chain SLH-DSA + STARK on Solana, not in-circuit) — gap.

5.2 **Falcon in a zkVM by design.** Polygon Miden ships RPO-Falcon512 for account authentication (hash-to-point with RPO256 instead of SHAKE256 so verification is cheap in Miden VM; public key = RPO digest of the key polynomial) and a Falcon512-Poseidon2 variant in miden-crypto 0.27–0.28. https://docs.rs/crate/miden-lib/0.12.4/source/src/account/auth/rpo_falcon_512_acl.rs , https://docs.rs/crate/miden-crypto/0.28.2/source/src/dsa/falcon512_poseidon2/mod.rs — `confirmed-primary` (source), note this is a non-standard Falcon variant.

5.3 **The alternative pattern — PQ-sign a salted-hash commitment, prove only hash membership + predicates — is the one ETSI endorses for mdoc/SD-JWT.** ETSI TR 119 476-1 §9.3: "In the case of salted attributes hashes, the main component vulnerable to quantum computers is the signature scheme used to sign the hash values. Transition to post-quantum cryptography will then mostly consist in replacing this signature scheme by a post-quantum counterpart such as the NIST standard FIPS 204"; multi-message schemes (BBS+/CL) instead need PQ variants "but also for the related zero-knowledge proof systems". Executive summary: hash-based NARKs "can flexibly adapt to new (e.g. post-quantum secure) signature schemes and novel (Q)EAA formats". §9.1 item 2: salted-hash privacy survives Grover by doubling the salt. — `confirmed-primary`. (In this pattern the issuer signature never enters the circuit when the verifier can see the signed MSO/root; a ZK proof is then needed only over the hash tree and predicates, and only *issuer-hiding/unlinkability* requirements force the signature back into the circuit.)

5.4 **What Longfellow actually does, and why it is not PQ as a credential system.** Frigo–shelat, "Anonymous credentials from ECDSA", ePrint 2024/2010 (Dec 2024, rev. Apr 2026, CiC vol. 3): proves ECDSA + SHA-256 + mdoc parsing in-circuit with a sumcheck/Ligero argument; ECDSA proof ≈ 20 ms, full mdoc presentation "a few hundred milliseconds" on mobile; the *argument* relies only on SHA-256. https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/2010 — `confirmed-primary`. IETF 124 secdispatch slides (Nov 2025): "optimized for statements on legacy cryptography (ECDSA and SHA-256) ... Compatible with existing, issued credential formats like mDOC"; deployed in Google Wallet (Bumble), ISRG Rust implementation underway, EUDI implementers (Multipaz, wwWallet) interested; outcome "I'm not sure". https://datatracker.ietf.org/meeting/124/materials/slides-124-dispatch-longfellow-zero-knowledge-credentials-00 — `confirmed-primary` (extracted text). CFRG slides same week report EU/Apple/Google feedback wanting to avoid new ECC schemes and prefer PQ, and that device binding and legacy support rule out the BBS family. https://datatracker.ietf.org/meeting/124/materials/slides-124-cfrg-longfellow-zk-01 — `secondary`. draft-google-cfrg-libzk-01 (2 Sep 2025) is an individual Informational draft, not adopted, Security Considerations silent on PQ. https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-google-cfrg-libzk-01.html — `confirmed-primary`. So: ZK layer PQ-plausible (hash-only), credential soundness bounded by ECDSA (1.4, ETSI).

5.5 **EUDI ARF Topic G (ZKP) — no PQ position yet.** Discussion paper v1.4 (30 Mar 2025): two families (multi-message signatures BBS+/BBS#; programmable zk-SNARKs, citing Crescent); post-quantum "not discussed"; HLRs: Req 6 proofs over already-issued ISO/IEC 18013-5 and SD-JWT VC attestations; Req 8 rely "solely on algorithms standardised by a standardisation organisation" recognised by the Commission; Req 9 ZKP "SHALL NOT prevent" LoA High. https://eudi.dev/3.0.0/discussion-topics/g-zero-knowledge-proof/ — `confirmed-primary`. Topic G is in a 2026 refinement round (GitHub discussion #700). https://github.com/eu-digital-identity-wallet/eudi-doc-architecture-and-reference-framework/discussions/700 — `secondary`. ETSI TS 119 476-2 (ZKP implementation in EUDI Wallet) and -3 (WUA/WIA) have planned publication 28 Feb 2027. https://github.com/eu-digital-identity-wallet/eudi-doc-standards-and-technical-specifications/issues/498 — `secondary`. ETSI ESI (2025 PKI Consortium talk): "only quantum-safe ZKP should be considered" but "there is currently no programme for identifying recommended quantum-safe ZKP algorithms". https://pkic.org/events/2025/pqc-conference-austin-us/THU_BREAKOUT_1000_I%C3%B1igo-Barreira_ETSI-ESI_QSC-PQC-conference.pdf — `secondary`.

5.6 **ENISA / BSI / ANSSI on PQ + ZKP for identity.** No ENISA, BSI or ANSSI document specific to PQ-ZKP for digital identity was found for 2025–26 (the 2025 ANSSI–BSI joint EUDI paper is on remote identity proofing). What exists: the 18-agency statement "Securing Tomorrow, Today: Transitioning to Post-Quantum Cryptography" (ANSSI/BSI/NL/SE +14, 30 Nov 2024): hybrid PQ/classical, most sensitive use cases protected "by the end of 2030 at the latest". https://cyber.gouv.fr/en/publications/jointly-led-international-publications/transitioning-to-post-quantum-cryptography/ — `confirmed-primary` (ANSSI page via search). BSI TR-02102-1 (2025 update) adds ML-KEM, ML-DSA, SLH-DSA alongside FrodoKEM/McEliece/XMSS/LMS; hybrid required for KEMs — `secondary`. ETSI TR 119 476-1 cites [i.2] (this statement) and NIST IR 8547 as its PQ anchors — `confirmed-primary`.

## Key findings (15)

1. Shor breaks the pairing groups under Groth16/KZG/BBS/PS; what breaks is **soundness/authenticity** (forgery, CRS-trapdoor recovery), not **zero-knowledge** — perfect/unconditional hiding survives a CRQC (BBS draft-12 §6.9 "everlasting privacy"; ETSI TR 119 476-1 §9.1).
2. Consequence: hidden attributes in old BBS/Groth16 presentations are safe forever; what is not safe is any *future* verification of a pre-Q-day proof or a verifier that accepts proofs after Q-day. ETSI: "harvest now, forge later" is a much smaller window than HNDL; mitigations are short-lived attestations, key rotation, timestamps/hash chains, then migration.
3. NIST IR 8547 (Nov 2024): ECDSA/EdDSA/RSA deprecated after 2030, disallowed after 2035; symmetric/hash primitives at ≥128-bit are Category 1 — so hash-based ZK is on the surviving side. BN254/BLS12-381 are not NIST curves at all.
4. Hash-based IOP SNARKs (STARK/FRI, Ligero, Aurora, STIR, WHIR, Brakedown/Binius) are unconditionally secure in the QROM when the IOP is round-by-round sound (Chiesa–Manohar–Spooner 2019); FRI's Fiat–Shamir security is proven (Block et al. 2023); Kilian is PQ in the standard model from collapsing hashes (CMSZ 2021).
5. The up-to-capacity Reed–Solomon proximity-gap conjectures were disproved Dec 2025–Apr 2026 (Crites–Stewart; Diamond–Gruen; Ben-Sasson et al.; Kambiré). Cost is parametric (≈2–3 % under the restated conjecture, ≈2× under proven bounds), worst for small/binary fields; a PQ profile should state which bound it relies on.
6. PQ proof sizes are 10^2–10^4 × Groth16's 128 bytes: WHIR ≈ 63 KiB per opening, LaBRADOR 58 KB, ProveKit ≈ 1 MB, zkVM STARKs 200–900 KiB, zkDilithium tokens 85–175 KB. Every production stack that needs on-chain verification (SP1, RISC Zero, Stwo, ProveKit) wraps in Groth16 and thereby loses PQ soundness.
7. Lattice anonymous credentials are real but research-grade: credentials ≈ 54–80 KB (Argo et al. CCS 2024), few-dozen-KB framework (Bootle et al. CRYPTO 2023), 22 KB 2-round blind signatures (Beullens et al.), all implementable via IBM's LaZer (CCS 2024) on LaBRADOR/LNP22. No IETF/W3C/ETSI standardisation track exists for PQ blind signatures or ACs (ETSI §9.4.1).
8. NIST signatures: ML-DSA (FIPS 204) and SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) final; FN-DSA (FIPS 206) still has no public draft (CSRC IPD URL 404 on 2026-10-12). On-ramp Round 3 (NIST IR 8610, 14 May 2026): FAEST, MAYO, MQOM, QR-UOV, SDitH, SNOVA, SQIsign, UOV (+HAWK, since withdrawn).
9. "ZK-friendly PQ signature" means two things: MPCitH/VOLEitH signatures that *are* NIZKs (FAEST, MQOM, SDitH — not succinct, not circuit-cheap) versus hash-based signatures with algebraic hashes verifiable cheaply in-circuit (zkDilithium, Miden RPO-Falcon, Ethereum XMSS/Poseidon). Nothing NIST is standardising is designed for the second.
10. Proving ML-DSA verification in-circuit costs millions of constraints / tens of seconds today (16.5 M R1CS for ML-DSA-87; ~22 s in SP1), SHAKE/NTT-dominated; no credible in-circuit SLH-DSA/Falcon numbers exist. Miden is the only production system with a (non-standard) lattice signature verified natively inside a STARK.
11. The pattern the EU ecosystem endorses is "PQ-sign the salted-hash commitment, keep the signature out of the circuit": ETSI TR 119 476-1 §9.3 says mdoc MSO / SD-JWT transition "will mostly consist in replacing this signature scheme by ... FIPS 204", while BBS+/CL need both PQ signatures *and* PQ ZK; hash-based NARKs "flexibly adapt" to new signature schemes.
12. Longfellow (Google) is PQ at the argument layer (Ligero/sumcheck, SHA-256 only) but proves ECDSA, so credential soundness is classical; IETF 124 dispatch (Nov 2025) ended with no venue decided, and CFRG feedback from EU/Apple/Google asked for PQ-preferred schemes with device binding, which excludes the BBS family.
13. EUDI ARF Topic G v1.4 (Mar 2025) has no PQ position; its HLRs require standardised algorithms only (Req 8), compatibility with already-issued ISO 18013-5 / SD-JWT VC (Req 6) and LoA High (Req 9); refinement round runs in 2026; ETSI TS 119 476-2/-3 due Feb 2027; ETSI ESI: "no programme for identifying recommended quantum-safe ZKP algorithms". No ENISA/BSI/ANSSI PQ-ZKP-for-identity document exists; the 18-agency Nov 2024 statement demands hybrid PQ for the most sensitive uses by end-2030.
14. Deadlines a spec can cite: EU roadmap (Jun 2025) 2026 start / 2030 high-risk / 2035 all; UK NCSC (Mar 2025) 2028 / 2031 / 2035; US NSM-10 2035, NIST 2030 deprecate / 2035 disallow, CNSA 2.0 2030–2033 by category, 2035 all NSS.
15. Standards-track PQ hooks already available for a credential spec: RFC 9964 (ML-DSA in JOSE/COSE), RFC 9901 (SD-JWT, hash-based so algorithm-agile), LAMPS composite ML-DSA (RFC Editor queue, Oct 2026), W3C Quantum-Resistant Cryptosuites FPWD (Jun 2026: ML-DSA/SLH-DSA/Falcon/SQIsign incl. -sd- variants, no unlinkability). Nothing at ToIP/DIF; W3C CCG PQ-BBS pseudonym work is stalled for lack of editors (Jan 2026).
