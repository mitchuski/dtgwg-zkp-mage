## 1. The question

Sharing references on a call is a disclosure act, and the lab's maintainer asked the honest
version of the question: not *what influenced this work* — everything did — but *what can be
cited*: named, linked, and followed by a stranger to a public artifact that actually carries
the claim. That framing does most of the work. Influence is cheap to assert; a citation is a
promise that the reader can go and check.

## 2. The citable ledger

Four ancestors qualified, each verified publicly reachable this morning, each carrying a
specific discipline the lab now runs on:

**The dual-agent harness** (github.com/mitchuski/agentprivacy-harness). The
proposer-and-independent-prover fold — an edge advances only when a proposal survives a
non-colluding check — is the harness's core pattern, and the lab's trust-graph-formation
runtime is built as exactly that fold. The verify-before-accept discipline that runs through
every suite is this pattern generalised. The cleanest citation in the set: the method predates
the task force.

**The Kyra gatehouse** (github.com/mitchuski/kyra_gatehouse). A Know-Your-Agent admission gate
built under hackathon pressure: a 214-assertion conformance suite, adversarial personas run
against the gate, interoperability exercised in both directions. The registry's
seat-admission discipline and the fixtures-first conformance habit both have their visible
ancestor here — and KY-A is native vocabulary in the room the citation is for.

**The kappa lane** (github.com/mitchuski/uor-kappa-mage). "Understanding as key" — the lineage
behind the publication rite: access that opens on demonstrated understanding rather than on
credentials. The rite the Chair just named as the PR-LIV shape applied to the maintainer's own
seat was not improvised for this repo; its ancestor was public before the rite existed, and
the repo's own `SPEC-DRAFT-proof-of-understanding-trust-task.md` is the bridge between them.

**The privacy-value model** (agentprivacy.ai/model). The moving ceiling, value as a function
of position *and time* — the frame behind the erosion-clock instrument and behind the
"for how long, as a rate not a cliff" language that today's substrate note leaned on. The
horizon discipline came from here.

## 3. The held-out ledger

Three lineages are real, load-bearing, and deliberately not cited:

- **The nullifier lineage.** The uniqueness runtime descends from earlier privacy-pools work
  that lives in private repositories. Spoken of freely, linked never — a citation that cannot
  be followed is an assertion wearing a citation's clothes.
- **The trust-task conformance specs.** Ten specs, green locally, uncommitted. These are the
  strongest future citation for ceremony-as-trust-task — and worth publishing before the
  Lane 2 collaboration starts in earnest — but today they do not exist to a stranger, so
  today they are not references.
- **The consent-federation work.** The context-card instrument's twin is a localhost
  federation of the MyTerms pattern; the citable object is the external standard
  (IEEE P7012), not the private implementation of it.

The symmetry is the day's finding. A few hours earlier, the substrate note had excluded
Drake's remarks about unpublished cryptanalysis as hearsay — real, possibly true, not
carriable until citable. The held-out ledger is the same rule pointed inward: **private and
uncommitted work is hearsay about oneself.** It gets no exemption for being yours.

## 4. Surfacing

The four citable ancestors went onto the call companion as a lineage strip — each with the
one-sentence account of what it carried into the lab — with a closing line stating the rule:
references limited to public artifacts; lineage that cannot be followed is named in kind but
not linked. The companion stays a page where every claim has a footable trace, including the
claims about where the lab came from.

## Why this is interesting

- **Citability is a disclosure boundary, applied to one's own past.** The lab's whole method
  is narrow claims with checkable evidence; a lineage list built any other way — influence
  without links, credit without traces — would be the one artifact in the repo that violates
  its own register.
- **The ancestors were public before the lab needed them.** Nothing was rushed out to
  decorate a citation list; the four references were pushed for their own reasons, months or
  days before the question arose. Lineage you don't have to manufacture is the only kind
  worth listing.
- **"Hearsay about oneself" is a usable test.** Would this reference survive the same
  scrutiny the lab applied to someone else's unpublished claims? If a stranger cannot follow
  it to the artifact, it fails — whoever owns it.
- **The held-out ledger is also a work queue.** Every uncitable lineage is one push away from
  citable, and the list says which push matters next: the trust-task specs, before Lane 2
  opens.
