The requirements from the 8 September note are carried in: record 010 on the [PR #8](https://github.com/trustoverip/dtgwg-zkp-spec/pull/8) branch (second commit `e1afc3f`, 14 September) now states that the offline membership check covers the community-issued grant half only, lists the composed proof's ingredients (credential authenticity, both parties' identifier linkage, status freshness, the shared witnesses), states the adversary, disclosure set and validity horizon per privacy claim, and keeps rejected witnesses distinct from verifier-side rejection tests. Its evidence state is unchanged; nothing is adopted.

On the offline linkage, the record names three routes rather than one, and each answers the question put above in a different place:

1. **Directed-identifier reuse under the declared scope.** The voucher uses one `directed` identifier both as the VRC's issuer and as its membership credential's subject. The relation the presenter then proves is hidden-value equality (record 009: the VRC's issuer field equals the membership leaf's subject field, across credentials signed by different parties), not common control. It needs no artifact from the voucher beyond the two credentials, and it costs the correlation the scope declares — which is the credential layer's default for intra-community edges.

2. **An issuance-time linkage proof carried by the VRC.** Where the voucher used pairwise identifiers, the VRC carries the voucher's own co-control attestation between its VRC-side and VMC-side identifiers — record 007 run in the voucher's hands at issuance. The credential specification's thread ([cred-spec #9](https://github.com/trustoverip/dtgwg-cred-spec/issues/9)) frames this as a MAY on the VRC; the artifact's format, its authenticated statement and its private composition into 010 are what still need specifying and testing. This is the version of the question for the issuance flow: whether the flow can produce that attestation at vouch time.

3. **The blind-signature vouch.** As framed on the call, the voucher blind-signs once and the presenter later proves possession of the unblinded signature under the community's key, so the voucher is never online at presentation. Record 010 carries it as a construction option marked conjecture, with the call as its source. Whether it *is* the linkage artifact turns on what the signed message binds: if it binds the relationship identifier to the vouching member's membership, it answers routes 1 and 2 at once; if it binds only "a member vouched", it answers the membership half and leaves the relationship half where it was. That, and how it composes with the common-control and non-revocation clauses, is the version of the question for the authors.

What the presenter cannot do on any route is manufacture the relation from its own secret — the record says so in its does-not-establish list, and a construction that reads a link out of a disclosed field would fail the non-correlation requirement in 007.

On the smallest representative implementation: the record's conformance vectors (`conformance/` on the branch) are the acceptance tests any candidate has to pass, and the reference lab's registry rows are component measurements for the Groth16 shape — evidence for components, not an end-to-end ADR-001. The first end-to-end candidate on any of the three routes would move 010 up the state ladder; the records that would need to change with it are 010, 007 and 009, and 012 if route 1 is chosen.
