The working draft in [PR #8](https://github.com/trustoverip/dtgwg-zkp-spec/pull/8) moved on 14 September: a second commit on `zk-book` (`e1afc3f`, 22 files) folds the review since the draft was opened on 8 September. This is what changed, what did not, and why the pull request is worth pulling in now rather than after Round 1.

**What changed.**

- **Two records revised against merged credential text.** 007 (common control) now covers identifiers a party holds as subject or as issuer, and no longer claims the chain predicates — those are a different relation, hidden-value equality across credentials signed by different parties, as the credential thread put it on 10 September ([cred-spec #9](https://github.com/trustoverip/dtgwg-cred-spec/issues/9)). 020 (delegation chain) is re-read against the merged VDC: depth bounded by every ancestor, acceptance required, status conditional on every credential in the chain.
- **Three records added.** 009, hidden-value equality, a new primitive with its own gadget (the dual of distinctness); 021, the VAC attenuation chain, 020's sibling for authority; 013, mutual edge admissibility ([cred-spec #25](https://github.com/trustoverip/dtgwg-cred-spec/issues/25)), at `requested` — a placeholder the state ladder allows and the text says so.
- **Records touched by the 8 September call and this week's credential threads.** 010 carries the blind-signature vouch as a construction option, marked conjecture with the call as its source, and states that vetting evidence ([PR #50](https://github.com/trustoverip/dtgwg-cred-spec/pull/50)) is not the voucher linkage. 008 cites the same proposal's salted commitment as an instance of its first route. 003 says that binding a predicate identifier does not make it accepted or meaningful ([#52](https://github.com/trustoverip/dtgwg-cred-spec/issues/52)).
- **Chapters.** Implementation Guide steps 4 and 5 (predicate identity before binding; evidence is not a decision), the integration chapter's request rules, the Research and Book chapter (notes of 10, 13 and 14 September), Security Considerations 5, Internationalization hedged to the open encoding decision, the conformance vector count corrected to the manifest, the references completed, and **Appendix E** — an index of every numbered working-group note, which is the list Round 1 is deciding.

**What did not change.** Every record keeps its evidence state; nothing rose. No construction or proving system is proposed as adopted. The generated sections regenerate from `conformance/` and match, the validator reports 20 files valid, and the specification still selects nothing and audits nothing. The editors line is as opened: one editor, the chair listed as contributor, with the seat open to him.

**Why pull it in now.** Three reasons, none of them about the text being finished. First, the draft is generated and checkable: a reader who distrusts the prose can read the twenty machine-readable records instead, run the validator, and see which claims the text is entitled to make — that is a property worth having on the task force's branch rather than in one contributor's clone. Second, the credential specification is citing it: its merged editor's note names the common-control primitive and the blinded binders as waiting on this task force, and three of its live threads (#9, #50, #52) now have record identifiers to point at — 007, 009, 020, 021 — which only resolve if the draft is where the working group can see it. Third, Round 1 is a shape review, and shape is cheap to change at fifteen records and expensive at fifty; pulling the draft in as the working draft is what lets the shape questions be decided on a text that will still be the text afterwards.

**Where to read.** The shortest path: the Implementation Guide, then one primitive record (001) and one composed record (010), then Appendix E. The [catalogue thread](https://github.com/trustoverip/dtgwg-zkp-spec/discussions/9) links each record by identifier; `npm install && npm run render` renders it locally. A position record in the shape the evidence repository's `PATH-MAP.md` describes — ratify, refine, refute, or build, on which item, with the reasoning bound by the drafting rules — remains the most useful form a reply can take.
