Three points, three answers; the record edits ride the Round 2 pull request.

**1. One construction, two rows.** Agreed, and the reading is confirmed from the framework side: the five Trust Tasks OpenVTC published on 23 September (`pcs-root`, `pcs-tokens`, `vetting/attestation`, `pcs-challenge`, `event-mode`) are the wire of a blind-signature admission, and record **024** (hidden-vetting admission) already carries the PCS as its option 1 with those tasks as its issuance lines. So the cross-reference goes the other way from the one the comment suggests: 023's options[0] and options[3] are both pointers to 024, and 023 stays what it is — the vouch-admission statement over k relationship credentials, whose constructions are Merkle-membership ones. The two rows are folded into one option on 023 that names 024 and says why the statement differs (024 admits on k distinct attestations from a hidden vetter set; 023 admits on k distinct vouching members of a known set; the applicant's own membership is outside both).

**2. The 007 bypass, stated.** Correct, and it belongs in the record's negative space rather than in a footnote. Under the PCS route the linkage between a vouch and the party it vouches for rests on a secret bound at enrolment inside the credential system, not on a commitment carried in the identifier's DID document. That means the identifier-commitment profile (WG-14) is not exercised by that route at all, and a community that admits over it gets no 007 property: nothing ties the proof to the applicant's DID beyond what the PCS credential itself binds. Both 023 and 024 gain a does-not-establish line saying exactly that, and 024's option 1 names it as the price of the route. Whether the task force wants 007 satisfied on the admission path, or is content with the credential-bound linkage, is a Round 2 question and is filed as such.

**3. Figures: the source is public now, so the record pins it and stops there.** `OpenVTC/predicate-credential-system` took its source on 28 September (main `4f4edf5`, MIT, with the `pcs-vetting-prototype` demo), so record 024's option 1 now names that revision and carries the sizes the repository asserts in its own tests: a 1,392-byte proof at k = 5 under Σ-PS with Tag_DDH, 240-byte attestations, 96-byte credentials. Those are recorded as constructor-reported, `measured: false`, because nobody on this side has run them; running times are not recorded at all, since the repository publishes benches but no figures, and the ~12 ms in the comment has no source the record can cite. The local run of the reference flow reported on 29 September (eleven steps, matching identifiers, labels and shapes) is useful corroboration and is noted; the commit it names is not on a public branch, so the record pins the public main rather than it. The state stays `constructed` until a run on independent hardware, which is the board's rule for every record.

The options were read as constructions rather than as prose, which is the review the records were written for.
